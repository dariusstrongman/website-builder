SEO / Search foundations

“SEO included” should mean more than installing a plugin.

A website build can create a strong technical and content foundation. Rankings still depend on competition, authority, useful content and work after launch.

10 minute guideUpdated September 2026No ranking guarantees
IN THIS GUIDEIncluded at launchOngoing growthMeasurementQuestions to ask

What every professional build should include

Semantic HTML and logical headingsUnique page titles and descriptionsDescriptive URLs and internal linksCanonical URLs where neededXML sitemap and crawl directivesUseful image alternative textStructured data when appropriateFast, responsive page deliveryCrawlable text for important claimsAnalytics and Search Console readiness

These items help search engines discover and understand the website. Google still recommends building helpful, reliable, people-first content rather than pages created primarily to manipulate rankings.

Primary reference: Google Search Central’s people-first content guidance.

What a website launch cannot finish

Search growth normally requires ongoing work: learning which questions buyers ask, publishing genuinely useful answers, earning relevant mentions, improving weak pages and measuring which visits turn into business. A sitemap alone does not create demand.

WE CAN PROMISEA clean, crawlable foundation and clearly documented launch work.
WE CANNOT PROMISEA specific ranking, traffic number or deadline controlled by a search engine.

Measure business outcomes, not vanity

  • Qualified organic visits to important pages
  • Search queries that match the actual offer
  • Form submissions, bookings or purchases from organic traffic
  • Indexing errors, page experience and crawl health
  • Content that assists a later conversion

Ask a website provider these questions

  1. Which SEO tasks are completed before launch?
  2. Who owns keyword and content strategy after launch?
  3. How will redirects be handled if URLs change?
  4. Which analytics and search tools will be connected?
  5. What is measured 30, 60 and 90 days later?
