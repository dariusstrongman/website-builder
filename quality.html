Nine release gates

No average hides
a serious failure.

A beautiful desktop hero cannot offset a broken mobile checkout. A fast site cannot offset invented proof. Material failures block release instead of disappearing inside one composite score.

01Message clarity

Can the intended buyer explain the offer and next step after a short exposure?

02Art direction

Does the visual system have an ownable thesis beyond competent styling?

03Originality

Would the site remain recognizable with the logo removed?

04Product communication

Do pages show how the offering works rather than hiding behind slogans?

05Conversion

Are objections, proof and calls to action ordered around the buyer’s decision?

06Mobile execution

Is the small-screen experience intentionally composed, readable and usable?

07Performance

Does production meet agreed Core Web Vitals and interaction budgets?

08Accessibility

Can keyboard, screen-reader and reduced-motion users complete key journeys?

09Claim integrity

Is every factual or comparative claim sourced, qualified or removed?

What automation cannot honestly certify

Remarkable still requires
people outside the build.

Code can detect contrast, broken links, missing labels and slow interactions. It cannot prove that a buyer trusts the company, remembers the idea or prefers the result to strong alternatives.

The final process therefore calls for blinded review from target buyers and independent design reviewers. Their feedback is evidence—not a guaranteed “10/10” badge.
