# Website Builder: benchmark-to-build standard

This is a project operating standard, not a claim that any site has automatically earned a score.
Updated 2026-09-05. Do not change prices or invent commercial proof to improve presentation.

## Core rule

**Derive the design from the business, its category, its audience and its proof. Never derive it from the previous site.**

The system should create websites that feel like different high-end agencies could have made them while holding the same quality floor.

## Current benchmark lessons

- Gamma: visual confidence, product-led storytelling, clear hierarchy and polished section rhythm.
- Clay: distinctive hero composition grounded by real product/proof.
- Tend: trust, calmness and premium visual reassurance.
- Linear / Vercel / Raycast / Attio: disciplined technical-product presentation.
- Stripe / Cursor / ElevenLabs / Ramp: dense product information made clear without losing polish.
- Drawer.ai: restraint, composure, product UI integrated into the narrative, clean long-page rhythm, technical clarity and selective motion.

See `BENCHMARK_LIBRARY.md` for the current reference library and the specific principle to transfer from each one.

## The build process

A premium customer build should follow this sequence:

1. **Truth first** — collect business facts, actual offer, audience, proof, constraints and primary conversion goal.
2. **Research** — study the category, buyer expectations, relevant references and what must not be copied.
3. **Creative divergence** — generate 2–3 directions that materially differ in composition, typography, media language, color environment, graphic logic, motion logic or density.
4. **Rendered concept studies** — judge images, not prose descriptions of imagined designs.
5. **Independent ranking** — the creator does not self-approve the winning direction.
6. **Direction lock** — once selected, do not drift into rejected sibling concepts.
7. **Proof slice first** — build the hero/key homepage slice before expanding the whole site.
8. **Premium floor checks** — craft, typography, repetition, slop, truth and direction consistency.
9. **Independent visual review** — score the rendered result on the canonical 100-point scorecard.
10. **Rework loop** — fix the actual weakest categories before full expansion.
11. **Full-site build** — preserve the approved visual system across internal pages.
12. **Browser QA** — desktop/mobile evidence, responsive behavior, keyboard, reduced motion, loading and errors.
13. **Final independent review** — the site must clear the score floor and category floors before delivery.

## Canonical score floor

Target ship bar: **92/100** with category minimums.

| Category | Max | Minimum |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Strategic clarity | 10 | 8 |
| Brand distinctiveness | 15 | 12 |
| Trust / truth / evidence | 15 | 13 |
| Typography / composition | 15 | 13 |
| Product storytelling | 10 | 8 |
| IA / conversion | 10 | 8 |
| Responsive execution | 10 | 9 |
| Interaction / motion | 5 | 4 |
| Accessibility | 5 | 5 |
| Performance / SEO | 5 | 4 |

Passing the total while failing a critical category is not a pass.

## Non-negotiable design decisions

1. **No generic house style for customers.** A health company, industrial contractor and software product must not look like recolored siblings.
2. **No fake maturity.** No fabricated testimonials, customer logos, usage counts, awards, ROI or fake dashboard data.
3. **Product or business artifacts should carry the design when they are strong.** Real UI, plans, maps, workflows, products, projects, documents, people or inventory should not be buried under decoration.
4. **One signature moment is better than constant spectacle.** A site does not need every section to be a visual trick.
5. **Ambition needs restraint.** Drawer.ai is the reference lesson here: when the actual product visual is strong, give it room instead of adding another decorative layer.
6. **Motion must explain something.** Reveal, state change, relationship, progress, confirmation or a justified signature moment. No animation exists merely to prove a library was installed.
7. **No-motion can be premium.** Pricing, legal, reading-heavy and trust-sensitive pages often benefit from restraint.
8. **Long-page consistency matters.** Composition should vary, but the grid, type system, spacing logic and component quality should remain coherent.
9. **Technical density should be organized, not hidden.** Use hierarchy, grouping and whitespace rather than deleting useful detail to make a screenshot look clean.
10. **Internal pages must hold the same standard as the homepage.** A great hero and generic pricing/about pages fail the product.
11. **Mobile is its own composition.** Do not merely stack desktop sections and call it responsive.
12. **The real journey must work.** Navigation, forms, CTAs, footer, 404/loading/error states and primary conversion path are part of design quality.

## Drawer.ai transfer rule

Use Drawer.ai as a quality reference when a build needs mature B2B/product composure:

- let actual product imagery dominate the section when it already explains value
- keep a disciplined grid across long pages
- vary layouts without losing spatial consistency
- make dense technical content feel calm
- use motion selectively to improve comprehension
- polish section transitions, not just hero moments
- reduce visual noise before adding more visual effects

Do **not** copy Drawer.ai's palette, electrical-industry motifs, component shapes or page layouts.

## Anti-AI-slop defaults

Avoid unless the brief specifically earns them:

- default purple/cyan gradients
- glowing blobs
- generic bento/card grids everywhere
- cards inside cards
- fake dashboards
- identical fade-up animation on every section
- giant centered template hero + gray subheading + two equal buttons
- random numbered sections where sequence has no meaning
- endless rounded rectangles
- scrolljacking
- excessive parallax
- decorative metrics with no truthful source

## Release blockers

A build cannot ship with:

- fabricated proof
- broken primary journey
- critical accessibility barrier
- unusable mobile layout
- deceptive or unlicensed assets
- generic creative direction with no business-specific rationale
- severe unexplained performance problem
- unresolved visual-review category floor failure

## Before the next release

- Compare actual rendered pages, not just source.
- Review the proof slice before expanding the whole site.
- Ask whether every major design moment is helping the business story or merely decorating the page.
- Recheck the entire customer path after shared CSS/navigation changes.
- Keep meaningful proof near the decisions it supports.
- State remaining limitations without claiming guaranteed conversion or a guaranteed 10/10.
