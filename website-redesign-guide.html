Planning / Redesign

Do not redesign because the website feels old. Redesign because it blocks the business.

A redesign should solve a specific decision problem. Diagnose the failure first so the new site does not become a more attractive version of the same confusion.

Start with the failed decision

What should a visitor understand or do that they currently do not? The answer might be “understand the service,” “trust the company,” “find the right product,” “book a consultation,” or “complete checkout on mobile.” That becomes the redesign objective.

Strong signals that redesign work is justified

Clarity failureVisitors cannot explain the offer after scanning the first screen.
Trust failureThe business is credible, but the site provides little evidence and feels unfinished.
Journey failureImportant actions are buried, confusing or broken on common devices.
Content failurePages answer internal company questions instead of buyer questions.
Search failureImportant services have no useful, crawlable page or logical internal path.
System failureEvery update creates inconsistency because no design or content system exists.

A redesign is not permission to erase everything

Keep URLs that already earn useful traffic when possible. Inventory analytics, forms, integrations, high-performing copy, customer questions and credible proof before changing structure. Plan redirects for URLs that must move.

A useful redesign brief fits on one screen

  • Primary buyer and the decision they are making
  • Main offer and the clearest next action
  • Top objections in the order buyers feel them
  • Proof available now—and proof that does not exist yet
  • Pages, integrations and content owners
  • Success measures after launch
